Five habits that help

You don’t need to be a technology expert

These habits work for phone calls, text messages, emails, and letters, whatever new tricks appear.

Practice these habits →
  1. Pause before you act. Scammers create a rush. A real bank, doctor, or family member can wait a few minutes while you check.
  2. Check using a number you already trust. Hang up and call the number on your card, statement, or the organization’s official website, not a number given in the message.
  3. Keep codes and passwords private. Never share a password, PIN, or one-time verification code with someone who contacted you.
  4. Be careful with unusual payments. Requests for gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or cash pickups are common signs of a scam.
  5. Talk it over. Ask a family member or friend before acting. Some families choose a private code word to confirm real emergencies.

If you think you’ve been scammed

It can happen to anyone, at any age. Acting quickly helps.

  • Call your bank or card company using the number on the back of your card.
  • Tell someone you trust what happened.
  • Change any passwords you may have shared.
  • In the United States, you can report fraud to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
